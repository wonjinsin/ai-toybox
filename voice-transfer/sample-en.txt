I used to think that every important decision had to happen in a hurry. You see an opportunity, you take it, and you figure out the rest along the way. Sometimes that works. Sometimes it just means you arrive in the wrong place a little faster.

Last summer, I drove out of the city before sunrise. No meetings, no schedule, and no particular destination. I stopped at a small diner beside the highway and ordered coffee. The man behind the counter asked where I was going. I told him I wasn't sure yet. He smiled and said, "Then you're probably having a better morning than most people."

I stayed there for almost an hour, watching the light come through the windows. Nothing remarkable happened. Nobody called with life-changing news. But for the first time in a long while, I didn't feel like I was running late for my own life.

Maybe that's the point. You don't always need a better plan. Sometimes you just need enough quiet to notice where you already are.
